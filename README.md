# Macroscope — operator's guide

*Version française : [README.fr.md](README.fr.md).*

Three static pages (the `macroscope` repository published by GitHub Pages), a small Google Apps Script "server" backed by a Google Sheet (decisions, results, pledges, comments), and **the OPEN-MU simulation engine** (`engine.js`, a stock-flow consistent model of a monetary union of N countries, with a fixed exchange rate against the rest of the world) that runs in the administrator's browser: no manual data entry, no third-party software.

| Page | For whom | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `desk.html` | the teams (link or QR code, no account) | sign in with the team's PIN, submit the warm-up plan (3 years) or the decisions of the year, read the results, comment, take part in the council of ministers |
| `admin.html` | you | open and close the rounds, publish a communiqué, **set the shocks and run the model** (*Run* tab), publish the results, ranking, council, CSV export |
| `board.html?class=…` | the projector | state of the round, countdown, communiqué, **the union's foreign reserves and alert**, ranking, pledges, "pledges vs decisions" |

Everything the students see is in English.

---

## 1. Setting up (once, about 15 minutes)

0. **Your copy of the site**: on GitHub, *Fork* the repository [`leomalherbe/macroscope`](https://github.com/leomalherbe/macroscope), then *Settings → Pages* → *Deploy from a branch* → `main` / `/ (root)`. The site goes live at `https://<account>.github.io/macroscope/`.
1. **Create an empty Google Sheet** (e.g. "Macroscope – 2026") from your Google account.
2. Menu **Extensions → Apps Script**. Delete the content of `Code.gs`, **paste the content of the repository's `Code.gs` file**, save (disk icon).
3. In the editor's toolbar, choose the function **`setup`**, then **Run**. Google asks you to authorise the script (account → "Advanced" → "Go to … (unsafe)" → Allow: it is your own script, the warning is normal). `setup` creates the tabs (Config, Groups, Decisions, Results, Pledges, Comments, Log) and generates the **administrator key**, shown in the Sheet's `Config` tab (and in the execution log).
4. **Deploy → New deployment** → type **Web app** → any description → *Execute as*: **Me** → *Who has access*: **Anyone** → **Deploy**. Copy the **Web app URL** (it ends with `/exec`).
5. In your copy of the repository: open `config.js` and paste this URL into `API_URL`, commit, push. (While `config.js` is empty, the "Student page" links and the PIN slips generated by the console carry the script's URL as an `?api=` parameter, so everything also works without this step.)
6. Open `admin.html` on your site (`https://<account>.github.io/macroscope/admin.html`), paste the URL and the key, **Connect**. Create the class (**+ New class**), then in the **Groups** tab → **Add 7 default groups** (Germany, France, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands, Belgium, Austria — the members of the model's union) → **Save groups**. Four-digit PINs are generated; **Print PIN slips** prints one slip per team with the link.

To test before the session: open the student page with a PIN on your phone, submit a plan, see it turn green in the **Run** tab, click **Run all submitted plans** then **Save and publish all**, and watch the results arrive on the phone.

> After any change to `Code.gs` (a new version of the backend): in the Apps Script editor, replace the whole content of `Code.gs` with the new file, save, then **Deploy → Manage deployments → ✎ (edit) → Version: "New version" → Deploy**. The URL does not change; nothing to redo on the site. Changing the HTML pages only needs a push. The console shows the script's version in its footer once connected (**v1.12** = two models, Trade and Finance, and the climate option (`sim.model` 'v1' / 'v2', `sim.climate`); v1.11 = the third choice of the reserves-crisis vote, a floating rate (models v1.3 and v2.0); v1.10 = tariffs (spec §13: vote on the common external tariff, tariff of a country that left); v1.9 = leaving the union (§12: the `leave` decision, the levers of a country that left, the "retaliation" round); v1.8 = arcade "duels" (classes of kind `duel`, the "policy rate" lever, pair rows `DUEL:p<k>`); v1.7 = sixth dimension "emissions" (column `emis` appended to the Results tab); v1.6 = the ecological levers of model v2.0; v1.5 = the crisis vote as a round of its own and the austerity cap checked by the backend; v1.4 = votes announced at the council; v1.3 = votes inside the decisions; v1.2 = shocks and "union" rows, without the votes); "Unknown action", or shocks that are not saved, mean the script has not been republished yet.

## 2. Running a session (checklist)

**Warm-up (every team governs France for 3 years, the other members stand still)**
1. *Run*: set the **shocks** of years 1 to 3 (world demand, central-bank rate, household confidence, import propensity, devaluation; 0 = no shock) → **Save shocks**. The same shocks apply to every team.
2. *Control*: write the communiqué (the shock, the time allowed), then **Open with timer** (10–15 min).
3. The teams submit their plan (three columns, years 1/2/3, relative changes in % of the reference bounded at ±10, four levers: public demand, social benefits, income tax, VAT) with their strategy stated beforehand (150 characters at least). They may change it until the round closes; only the last submission counts. Nobody sees the others' decisions while the round is open.
4. **Close submissions**, then *Run* → **▶ Run all submitted plans**: each plan is simulated alone (France follows the team's plan, the other members keep their reference policy). The preview shows the five deviations, R and the split own policy / foreign policies / shocks; **Save and publish all** (or **Save as draft** to check first in *Results*). Each team then sees its three years with the "of which" lines, the state of the union (reserves, current account, exchange rate) and writes its analysis afterwards.

> The warm-up's shocks and the game's are **separate**: when you move to the game, the shocks editor of the *Run* tab starts from zero for year 1 (the warm-up's shocks stay recorded on their own).
5. For the debrief: *Control* → tick "show every group's warm-up results and plans to everyone": the comparison table appears on the students' pages and on the board.

**The game (one country per team, year by year)**
1. *Control* → **Start the game → year 1** (submissions are closed). *Run*: shocks of year 1 → **Save shocks**. Communiqué, then **Open submissions** / **Open with timer**.
2. Whenever you like: **Convene the council**. Pledges are public (students' pages + board); decisions stay private. A pledge is a sentence, to which the team may attach figures (levers) and/or **the vote it announces** at the central bank (cut / hold / hike, and austerity / devaluation during an alert); with neither figures nor an announced vote, the chip "words only" is shown. **Adjourn** when the negotiation is over.
3. **Close submissions**: as soon as the round is closed, every country's decisions and votes become visible (students' pages + board). *Run* → **▶ Run year N** (refused while the round is open): the model replays years 1 to N from the recorded decisions, shocks and votes (a country without a decision keeps its reference policy and votes hold / austerity) → preview, with the tally of the votes and their effect → **Save and publish all**: the ranking and the union panel are updated.
4. *Control* → tick **Reveal pledges vs decisions for year N**: the "pledges vs decisions" table (✓ kept / ✗ broken) appears everywhere; it compares the announced figures with the levers decided, and the announced vote with the actual vote.
5. **Next year → N+1**, and start again. The students see their results and their past decisions, then justify the next year.

**Votes (game only).** With its yearly decisions, each country votes; one country, one vote; a majority of the playing countries is needed (strictly more than half of the teams with a country; a team that does not submit votes "hold" and "austerity"). Votes stay secret while the round is open and are revealed with the decisions when it closes.
- **The central bank's rate** (every round): *cut / hold / hike*. A majority of cuts lowers the rate by 0.5 point, a majority of hikes raises it by 0.5 point; **the change is permanent** (it adds up from one round to the next, until the next vote) and comes on top of the administrator's rate shock of the year; the rate stays between 0 and 10 %. The rate acts the following year, on investment and on every budget's debt service.
- **Reserves crisis — a round of its own, BEFORE the decisions.** When the union ended the previous year below the alert line (25 % of the initial stock), the *Control* tab shows a card "Reserves crisis · year N": **▶ Open the crisis vote** (submissions of decisions stay closed; the students' page only shows the question austerity / devaluation) → once everyone has voted, **■ Close the crisis vote and announce** (one country, one vote; absent teams do not count; devaluation needs more than half of the countries). **Finance model: a third choice, a floating rate**; the three choices form a continuum (austerity < devaluation < floating) and the median vote wins (with three votes, the middle one; with an even number, the lower median). The outcome is shown on the card, on the students' pages and on the board; put it in the communiqué, then **▶ Open submissions**. Austerity → the public demand and social benefits of **every** member are capped at −5 % of the reference for the year: the form and the backend refuse more. Devaluation → +10 % on the exchange rate at the year's Run. Floating rate → no cap; the regime is set to floating in that year's shocks and stays so in the following years (the reserves are frozen, the currency-crisis rules apply). *Reopen the vote* lets the teams vote again (decisions already submitted stay). Reserves exhausted **and a current account still in deficit** (R ≤ 0 and CA < 0) → no vote, the card offers **Record the forced devaluation** (15 %); as soon as the current account is back in surplus, there is no more forced devaluation and the crisis question comes back. The Run replays every year with the recorded outcome; if it finds a year that should have had a crisis round that was not recorded, it says so in the preview. Starting point: 3 months of the union's imports from the rest of the world (100 % of the gauge); with no reaction, a permanent −10 % shock on world demand leaves 69 % → 37 % → 7 % → exhausted; a yearly deficit larger than the remaining stock skips the alert altogether (e.g. 69 % left, then a year at −25 %).
The *Run* preview and the union panel (console, students' page, board) show the tally (x cut · y hold · z hike → ±0.5 pt · effective rate; x austerity · y devaluation → outcome; countries capped).

**Reserves and devaluation.** The exchange rate is fixed: the common central bank covers the union's current-account deficit with its reserves (3 months of imports at the start). The union panel (console, students' page, board) shows the reserves in months of imports and in % of the initial stock; below **25 %**, the **ALERT** label appears and the next round carries the austerity / devaluation vote. You may also decree a **devaluation** yourself: *Run* → field "Devaluation" of the year (e.g. 10 = the exchange rate jumps by 10 %) → **Save shocks** → announce it in the communiqué (it adds to a voted devaluation, if any). Effects: imports become dearer at once (imported inflation), exports recover with a one-year lag (J-curve).

The **board** (`Open the board ↗` in the console, or the **Board** button at the top of the students' page) refreshes itself every 15 s; the students' page every 30 s (there is also a Refresh button). At the bottom of the board, a folded section **Explore the model** draws any of about thirty variables (households, firms, government, trade, money, the union, the climate with its option) for the countries ticked, year by year, as deviations from the reference — for the debrief. The variables are recorded with each published year (years published before this tool: publish them again to fill the gaps).

### Beginners' guide (on by default)

On the students' page, a **How it works?** button opens a side panel: short cards on the mechanisms at work (the multiplier and leakages, VAT, the central bank's rate, reserves, devaluation, coordination, the « of which » split; then, by model version, price competitiveness, capital flows and floating rates, risk premium, the climate; leaving the union and tariffs when switched on; the duel), titles first, one card open at a time, and a glossary in its own tab. Small ⓘ buttons next to the levers, votes and cards open the matching card. The cards give the direction of effects only, never their size nor what to do. Switch it off for a class in *Control → Parameters* (« beginners' guide on the desk »), e.g. when you grade the justifications without help. Text in `guide.js` (English and French).

### Elections (every model, no backend change)

In the *Run* tab (game), under the shocks editor, the card **Elections · voters' preferences from year N** simulates a change in voters' preferences: new weights of the objective function R for **one country** or for **the whole union**, from year N until the next election (a national election prevails over a union-wide one). Two presets — *Left: unemployment first* (0.40 on unemployment, 0.15 elsewhere) and *Right: public finances first* (0.40 on the public balance) — and *Back to the class weights*; the five weights can also be set freely. Recorded in the shocks of the year (`elections`), so replayed like the other shocks: the Run computes the R of each country-year with the weights in force and stores them with the result; the students' page shows "Your objective this year" in the form, the chart of contributions uses each year's weights, the board lists the countries whose weights differ. The ranking is still the sum of the R. Announce the election in the communiqué.

**Sixth dimension (climate option): emissions.** In a class with the climate option, R may include the country's emissions (deviation in % from the reference, counted negatively). Its weight is **0 by default**: the damage already shows in unemployment and inflation, as a common good. It is switched on by an election (preset "Green: emissions first": emissions 0.40, the five others 0.12, for one country or the whole union) or by changing the class weights in Parameters. While it is zero, cutting one's emissions is a public good: a cost for whoever does it, a benefit for everyone.

### Leaving the union (Finance model, backend v1.9 needed)

Specification: `docs/openmu-spec.md` §12. Off by default; nothing changes for a class that does not use it (without an exit, the engine is identical bit for bit).

- **Switch on**: *Control → Parameters* → tick **leaving the union allowed (Finance model)** → *Save parameters*. Refused (and left unticked) if the class is not on the Finance model.
- **Announcement**: in its form, a team chooses "LEAVE the union at the end of this year" (asks for confirmation; irreversible). The announcement stays secret until the round closes, like the decisions. When year N is published, the console records `exits = {country: N+1}` in the class state: the country is out of the union from year N+1.
- **Shocks aimed at the leaving country** (*Run*, under the shocks editor, from the year of the announcement): its households' confidence (%), the rest of the world's demand for its bills (%, a one-year shock like "capital flows"), **deposit flight** (points of wealth, lasting; in the year of the announcement it is paid by the **common** reserves; a negative value brings the money back).
- **Council on retaliation** (*Control*, card "Council on retaliation against X"): offered automatically in the year of the exit, before the decisions; *Open* closes the submissions, the remaining members vote from their page (tariff of 0/5/10/20 % on X's exports, sale of 0/25/50/100 % a year of X's bills held by the union's central bank and, if X's central bank owes the union, immediate settlement of the TARGET balance); *Close and announce* takes the **median** of the votes (absent = 0). The measures stay in force until a new council ("Convene a council on X" in later years); *Open submissions* is refused while a council is sitting.
- **The country that left**: lever "Policy rate" (±2 pt), block "Your currency" (floating or pegged to the euro, change of the parity ±20 %, buy-back by its central bank of the bills the union sells), no more votes at the council or in a crisis; panel "Your currency" in its results.
- **Run preview**: lines "X announces its exit" and "X (out of the union): float/peg, …"; the union panel, the teams' page and the board list the countries out and the measures in force. The union's aggregates (GDP, reserves) cover the remaining members.

Orders of magnitude (France, Finance model, years 2 to 5 after an announcement in year 1): its rate cut by 1 pt in a float → depreciation of 11 %, inflation +2.6 pt, GDP −2.3 % then +1.4 %; a stimulus of +5 in a peg → reserves at 90, 78, 64, 47, 29 % of its share; a union tariff of 10 % → GDP −1.9 %; the union selling half of its bills → depreciation of 12 %. Tests: the EXIT block of `dev/engine-test.js`, `dev/e2e-exit.js`.

### Number of countries in the union (2 to 7)

The members of the union are the countries assigned to the teams in the **Groups** tab: at each **Save groups** (before the game), the union becomes the set of these countries — 2 to 7; a box "The union of this class: N members" says so. A country with no team is not in the union. The warm-up country is brought back into the union if it is not a member. **Once year 1 of the game has been run and saved, the union is fixed**: you can still rename a team or change a PIN, not add or remove a country. Order of magnitude: a French stimulus of +10 spreads twice as much to its partners in a France–Italy–Spain union as in the union of seven.

### Tariffs (backend v1.10 needed)

Specification: `docs/openmu-spec.md` §13. Off by default; can be switched on during a game (with no tariff, nothing changes).

- **Switch on**: *Control → Parameters* → tick **tariffs** → *Save parameters*.
- **Common external tariff**: each member votes 0, 5, 10 or 20 % with its decisions (in the votes block, the level in force is proposed by default); the median of the votes cast applies to the year; with no vote, the level stays. Shown in the Run preview, the union panel and the board.
- **The rest of the world answers**: shock *Rest of the world: tariff on union exports* (%) in the shocks editor, to set when the common tariff rises. When leaving the union is allowed, a second shock, *Rest of the world: tariff on the exports of a country out of the union* (%), does the same for the countries that left: blank (the default), the rest of the world treats them like the union; a value (0, for instance) makes an asymmetric shock.
- **A country that left**: lever *Tariff on goods from the union* (0–20 %) in its form; a line in its "Your currency" panel.
- Orders of magnitude: a common tariff of 10 % → revenue ≈ 1.4 % of GDP, public balance +1.2 pt, trade balance +1.1 pt, GDP −0.6 % (households pay it); with a retaliation of 10 % → GDP −2 %. Italy, having left, taxing the union at 10 % → revenue +1.6 pt of GDP, GDP +0.8 %, German GDP −0.6 %.

### Arcade mode: duels (class of kind `duel`, backend v1.8 needed)

A fast variant, with no warm-up and no council: **two identical countries, A and B**, each with its own currency, central bank and government, trading only with each other (no rest of the world), lending to each other (capital flows) and sharing a closed climate (the pollution stock is fed only by their two emissions). A class holds **N pairs played in parallel**: every pair is its own world, the shocks of the year apply to every pair, one *Run* simulates all the pairs. The model is set (two complete Finance + Climate economies, the partner replacing the rest of the world).

- **Create the class**: *+ New class* → name, then at the second question "Kind of class" answer `duel`. The class starts directly in the game, year 1, round closed; no warm-up, no council, no crisis vote.
- **Teams**: *Groups* tab → **Add N pairs** (creates "Pair 1 · Country A", "Pair 1 · Country B", …). A team's country is `p<k>:A` or `p<k>:B`; it can also be chosen from the list.
- **Levers**: the six of Finance + Climate (spending, transfers, taxes, carbon tax, green investment) **plus the policy rate** (`rate`, in points around 3 %, ±2; step 0.25). No vote on the rate: each player sets its own. An old backend refuses this lever ("belongs to the duel mode").
- **Exchange-rate regime** (shock `Exchange-rate regime`, per year, every pair): **fixed** — both central banks defend the parity; the country with a balance-of-payments deficit loses reserves (3 months of imports at the start) and the surplus country gains them; alert below 25 %; reserves exhausted with a continuing current-account deficit → **forced devaluation of 15 %** of that currency, automatic, the following year. **Floating** — the bilateral rate e (A's currency per unit of B's) balances the balance of payments and the reserves no longer move. The administrator can also decree a **devaluation of A or B** (shocks `devalA`, `devalB`, fixed regime only).
- **Shocks** (every pair): confidence, import propensity, **demand in A / demand in B** (% of public demand, to break the symmetry: a disaster −, a boom +), regime, devaluations, central-bank backstop (each country has its own), climate disaster. No world-emissions shock (there is no rest of the world).
- **Elections**: scope "Every player", "Every Country A", "Every Country B" or one country.
- **Run year N**: compact preview per pair (both countries + the pair row: e, regime, reserves of A and B, CA′, pressure, S, D), then *Publish*. The *Results* tab lists both countries of each pair.
- **Students' page**: seven levers, no vote; after publication, card "Your duel · pair k" (regime, e and its change, reserve bars of both countries, policy rates, climate), the partner's table, four "of which" lines (your own policy / **your partner's policy** / the shocks / the climate), both charts side by side, ranking of every player with you and your partner highlighted, decisions of the pair only.
- **Board**: a grid of pairs (one panel per pair) + the overall ranking.

Orders of magnitude (year 1, A alone raises spending by +10 %, fixed): GDP A +2.7 %, GDP B +1.0 % (through A's imports), A's reserves 93 % then 84, 74, 64, 52, 40, 28 % → alert in year 8 (3 months of reserves, cross-holdings of 15 % of GDP). In a float, the same stimulus depreciates A's currency by 3.9 % in year 1 and 23 % in year 5. A rise of 1 point in A's rate in a float appreciates its currency by about 7 %: GDP A +1.8 % then −1.7 %, B −1.9 % then +0.3 %. A carbon tax of 5 points in A alone cuts its emissions by 70 % in five years and the shared damage from 7 to 5.5 %: A pays, both benefit.

**Tests**: `node dev/engine-test.js` (DUEL block: stationarity, accounting identities and the bilateral balance of payments over 20 seeds × 20 years, forced devaluation) and `node dev/e2e-duel.js` (full scenario on the mock server).

## 3. Parameters (*Control* tab → *Parameters*)

| Parameter | Default | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Bounds: levers | ±10 % | **the four levers are relative changes, in %, of the country's own reference** (0 = no change); checked by the page and by the server. Productivity (TFP) shock: left off |
| Warm-up: country, years, instruments allowed, max. number of instruments, min. text | France, 3, the four levers, 4, 150 characters | an instrument "used" = non-zero in at least one year |
| Game: max. number of instruments, min. text, number of years | no limit, 100 characters, 5 | an "all zeros" decision is accepted in the game (doing nothing is a strategy: free riding) |
| Visibility of decisions | hidden while the round is open; visible to all once it is closed | box "once a round is closed, show everyone's decisions" in *Control*; the council's pledges are public by design, the justifications stay private |
| Weights of the objective function | 0.2 everywhere | R = Σ weight × deviation, negative signs on unemployment and inflation; computed by the console from the model's five deviations |
| Model | **v1 · Trade** for every new class: the monetary union with price levels in trade (one-year lag), a fixed exchange rate, reserves 3 months of imports. **v2 · Finance**: Trade + capital flows, fixed or floating rate per year, risk premium, central-bank backstop (below) | Control → Parameters → "Model and options"; a game in progress must keep its model (the Run replays every year with it); `state.sim.model` = `v1` or `v2`. Classes created before keep their former id and replay unchanged: v1.3 and v2.0 show as v2 · Finance (identical engine; saving the parameters turns v2.0 into v2 + climate), v1.1 (no price channel) and v1.2 (reserves 1.5 months) are listed for those classes only |
| Climate option | off | box "climate option" in Control → Parameters, on either model; `state.sim.climate` (see below) |
| Households option | off | box "households option" in Control → Parameters, on either model; `state.sim.households` (see below) |
| Votes | on; step 0.5 pt; austerity cap −5 %; voted devaluation 10 %; forced devaluation 15 %; (Finance, floating rate) currency crisis if the yearly depreciation exceeds 10 %, "defend" = +1 pt on the rate | box "votes enabled" to play without the votes; stored in `state.sim` |
| Model **v2 · Finance** (capital flows) | to choose in Control → Parameters → Model and options | the rest of the world holds union bills (15 % of GDP), households hold foreign bills (≈ 7 % of their wealth); initial reserves 3 months, as in Trade (the 1.5 months of the former v1.2 could not stand capital flows: a voted rate cut emptied the reserves in a year, a devaluation pumped them up to 300 %); **exchange-rate regime per year** in the shocks editor (it stays as set until you change it) (*Exchange-rate regime*: fixed = the reserves absorb, alert and crisis vote as before; float = reserves frozen, the exchange rate balances the balance of payments; the regime can change during the game, the parity when returning to fixed is the last floating rate); extra shocks: *Capital flows / sudden stop* (%, −10 = flight to quality; in a fixed regime −5 costs a fifth of the reserves and −30 empties them, in a float −30 ≈ +21 % depreciation (−20 ≈ +13 %); to repeat each year if the shock lasts), *World interest rate* (pt), *Central-bank backstop* (ticked by default: unticked, the central bank stops buying the bills of members indebted beyond 110 % of GDP, whose rate rises until households and banks absorb their debt); automatic risk premium beyond 110 % of debt (1 pt at 130 %), paid on the bills held by the private sector and abroad, with foreign holders fleeing. In a float, the "Currency crisis" card replaces the reserves card (vote *defend* / *let it float* before the decisions). Orders of magnitude on the page "The model". |
| **Climate option** (emissions and damages) | Control → Parameters → box "climate option", on Trade or Finance (**backend v1.12 needed**) | production emits (carbon intensity), a world pollution stock builds up (the rest of the world emits as much as the union) and damages the economy through the two thermometers: structural unemployment (+2 pt at 5 % of damage) and inflation on a reduced potential — with no reaction, damage ≈ 5 % in year 6; the split gains a fourth line "of which the climate"; two extra levers (0 to 10): **carbon tax** (budget revenue, inflation the year it changes, −4 % of carbon intensity per point the next year) and **green investment** (public spending that supports demand, but 60 % imported — about a third less effect on GDP than ordinary public spending, and a worse trade balance — with diminishing returns on carbon intensity: 2 units −7 %, 5 units −12.5 %, 10 units −17 % a year) — added automatically to the warm-up's instruments when the option is switched on; shocks: world emissions (%), climate disaster (points of damage); old backends refuse these levers |
| **Households option** (modest and wealthy households) | Control → Parameters → box "households option", on Trade or Finance (**backend v1.13 needed** to store inequality) | two halves of the population: the modest half (35 % of wages and all the benefits, no income tax, spends 90 % of its income) and the wealthy half (the rest of wages and all capital income, all the income tax, spends 60 %), with the same reference as one sector. **Inequality** (the wealthy half's share of disposable income, 54.8 % at the reference, as a gap in points) becomes a dimension of R, with a weight of 0 until the administrator (Parameters) or an election (preset « Equality ») gives it one; shown on the desk, the board and the fact sheets |
| Shocks (*Run* tab) | 0 | world demand (%), central-bank rate (points), household confidence (%), import propensity (%), devaluation (%; fixed regime only); in Finance: exchange-rate regime, capital flows, world rate, backstop; with the climate option: world emissions, climate disaster; recorded per year in the class state |

R = a₁·ΔGDP − a₂·Δunemployment − a₃·Δinflation + a₄·Δpublic balance + a₅·Δtrade balance, deviations from the model's stationary reference: GDP in %, unemployment and inflation in points (Okun and Phillips thermometers placed on the model), public and trade balances in points of GDP. R is the **score of the year** (the year's deviations, weighted), not a change from one year to the next; the **cumulative R** is the sum of the yearly scores ("Cumulative R" / "Cum. R" columns everywhere, with the same split own / foreign / shocks). The game's ranking and the warm-up debrief sort on the cumulative R. The split "own / foreign / shocks" comes from counterfactual simulations (shocks only; shocks + the country's own policy alone; everything).

The four levers (`core.js`, table `LEVERS`, included in `Code.gs`): public demand G, social benefits TR, income-tax rate θ, consumption tax τ. The model and its calibration are described in `docs/openmu-spec.md`.

Several classes: **+ New class**; each class has its own teams, state and board (`board.html?class=ID`).

## 4. Troubleshooting

- *Invalid administrator key*: the key is in the Sheet's `Config` tab (row `adminKey`).
- *Cannot reach the server* on the students' side: `API_URL` missing in `config.js`, or deployment not public ("Who has access: Anyone").
- The pages also work without `config.js` filled in: add `?api=SCRIPT_URL` to the address (useful to test before pushing).
- The Sheet is the truth: everything is append-only (the last row per team and round wins), the `Log` tab records every action. You may look at it during the session without breaking anything; avoid writing in it by hand.
- A team lost its PIN: *Groups* tab of the console.
- Delete a class (e.g. a test class): button **Delete class…** in the console's top bar; you must type the exact name of the class. Its teams, PINs, decisions, results, pledges and analyses are removed from the Sheet (the other classes are untouched). Irreversible.
- "engine not loaded" in the *Run* tab: `engine.js` is missing from the site or did not load; reload the page. Results can still be typed by hand in *Results*.
- "Close the round first": the Run year button refuses to run while submissions are open (*Control* → **Close submissions**).
- Shocks are not saved, or votes are refused / missing from the decisions: the Apps Script is older than v1.3 (see the console's footer); votes announced at the council disappear from the pledges: script at v1.3, v1.4 is needed; the crisis vote ("The crisis vote is not open") and the austerity cap need v1.5; paste `Code.gs` again and publish a new version.
- The question "austerity or devaluation" does not appear for the students although the union is under alert: the previous year was not **published** (Save and publish all), or the page has not been refreshed.
