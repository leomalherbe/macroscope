In one paragraph

Seven countries share one currency and one central bank; the rest of the world is a given (its demand for the union's exports, its price level). Each country is the chapter-6 open economy of class 5, with firms that invest on bank credit added: households spend out of income and wealth, governments spend, tax and borrow, imports leak in proportion to output. Inside the union there is no exchange rate; with the rest of the world the rate is fixed, so the central bank's foreign reserves absorb whatever the union's current account does not balance. Unemployment and inflation are read on the model (Okun, Phillips); since v1.2 the inflation differences between members accumulate into price levels that enter trade with a one-year lag — a country that inflates more than its partners loses competitiveness inside the union and with the rest of the world (the exchange rate is the only other price channel). The reference state is stationary: with no decisions and no shocks, every variable stays where it is, and every result you see is a deviation from that path.

Notation: X₋₁ is last year's value, X* the reference (stationary) value. Everything is real except trade with the rest of the world, where the import price in union currency is the exchange rate xr. Country subscripts are dropped when no confusion is possible.

The accounts: who pays whom, who owes whom

OPEN-MU is a stock-flow consistent model: every flow leaves one sector and reaches another, every financial asset is another sector’s liability. The two matrices below are the whole model’s accounting, for the union as a whole — the members added up, the common central bank, the rest of the world — with the values the engine computes, in % of the union’s GDP. Trade between members nets out at this level: only trade with the rest of the world is left.

Transactions-flow matrix

Sign convention: + a source of funds (a receipt, or more of a liability), − a use of funds (a payment, or more of an asset). Each row is one transaction seen from both sides and sums to zero; each column is one sector’s budget constraint and sums to zero. Interest is paid at last year’s rates on last year’s stocks (₋₁). In the reference year every change in stocks is zero.

Balance-sheet matrix (end of year)

Sign convention: + an asset, − a liability. Each financial row sums to zero; only real capital is net wealth for the union and the world together. Households’ net worth is their wealth V, the governments’ is minus their debt B.

Households

YD = YF + r₋₁·(Bh₋₁ + M₋₁) + TR − T_incdisposable income
T_inc = θ·(YF + r₋₁·(Bh₋₁ + M₋₁))income tax
CE = α1·(1 + confidence/100)·YD + α2·V₋₁consumption spending (including the consumption tax)
C = (1 − τc)·CEconsumption of goods
V = V₋₁ + YD − CEwealth
Bh = V·min(λcap, λ0 + λ1·r)government bills held
M = (V − Bh)·ν ; H = (V − Bh)·(1 − ν)bank deposits ; cash
The restoring force. Households aim at a wealth target: when income rises they save part of it, wealth grows, consumption out of wealth catches up, until YD = CE again. That is why a one-off stimulus fades and a permanent one settles at a new level. A higher rate makes them hold more bills (and earn more interest) — a mildly expansionary side of the rate.

Firms and banks

I = I*·max(0.5, 1 − ι·(r₋₁ − r*))investment, reacts to last year's rate
AF = δ·K*amortisation funds retained by firms
K = K₋₁ + I − AF ; L = L₋₁ + I − AFcapital ; bank loans (L = K)
YF = Y − AF − r₋₁·L₋₁income distributed to households
Bb = max(0, M − L) ; A = max(0, L − M)banks: surplus deposits placed in bills / advances from the central bank
Horizontalist banks. Firms borrow what they need to invest; banks lend it and refinance at the central bank if deposits fall short. The rate acts on investment the following year, with ι = 12: +1 point on the rate cuts investment by 12 %, about −1.2 % of GDP. This is the contractionary channel of the rate — stronger than the interest-income channel above.

Government and the common central bank

T = T_inc + τc·CEtotal taxes
Fcb = r₋₁·(Bcb₋₁ + A₋₁)central-bank profit, rebated to the government
B = B₋₁ + G + TR + r₋₁·B₋₁ − T − Fcbgovernment debt (bills)
Bcb = B − Bh − Bbthe central bank buys what nobody else holds
PB = T + Fcb − G − TR − r₋₁·B₋₁public balance (shown in points of GDP)
No market discipline inside the union: the central bank absorbs the bills households and banks do not want, at the common rate. The budget cost of a rate rise comes the following year (debt service on B₋₁ at last year's rate; public debt is about 105 % of GDP at the reference). Public debt ratios are reported, not constrained.

Trade: inside the union and with the rest of the world

IM = μ·(1 + imports/100)·Yimports in volume
IMU_ij = (1 − sR)·IM_i · s_ij·(Prel_j₋₁/Prel_i₋₁)^(−εU)imports of i from partner j: size share s_ij, times last year's relative price (dearer at home → more imports); v1.1: s_ij only
IMR = sR·IM·(xr₋₁/Prel₋₁)^(−ε)from the rest of the world (exchange rate and own price level, one-year lag)
IMV = Σ_j IMU_ij + xr·IMRvalue of imports in union currency
X = Σ_j IMU_ji + XRexports = partners' imports from me + exports to the rest of the world
XR = xR*·(1 + world/100)·(xr/Prel₋₁)^ηexports to the rest of the world (dearer at home → fewer exports)
Y = C + G + I + X − IMVoutput, solved simultaneously for the seven members
TB = X − IMVtrade balance (shown in points of GDP)
Leakages and competitiveness. μ = 0.30: thirty cents of every euro of demand go to imports, half of them to union partners (your stimulus is their export) and half to the rest of the world (lost for the union's reserves). Since v1.2 a member whose prices rise faster than its partners' imports more and exports less, one year later and cumulatively: a stimulus erodes itself (France alone, public demand +10 %: GDP +2.4 % in year 1, +1.4 % in year 5; trade balance −0.7 → −1.3 pt), austerity is an internal devaluation. After a devaluation the import bill rises first (prices move at once, volumes with a year's lag — the J-curve), then exports (η = 0.7) and imports (ε = 0.7) adjust: 0.7 + 0.7 > 1, the Marshall–Lerner condition holds.

The union and its reserves

CA = Σ_i (XR_i − xr·IMR_i)current account of the union with the rest of the world
R = R₋₁ + CA/xrforeign reserves, in world currency
xr = xr₋₁·(1 + deval/100)fixed rate: moves only by a (voted or forced) devaluation
NWcb = NWcb₋₁ + (xr − xr₋₁)·R₋₁the central bank's valuation gain on a devaluation
Σ_i H_i = Σ_i (Bcb_i + A_i) + xr·R − NWcbthe central bank's balance sheet closes every year
R0 = 1.5 months of RoW imports ; alert if R < 0.25·R0initial stock (v1.2; 3 months in v1.1) and alert line
A shared resource. Reserves are the union's, not any member's. Everybody's current account drains or fills the same stock, and the devaluation that follows an exhaustion hits every member's import prices. Orders of magnitude (v1.2): a world-demand shock of −5 % costs about 30 % of R₀ per year if nobody reacts, −10 % about 63 %.

Unemployment and inflation — two thermometers

gap = 100·(Y/Y* − 1)output gap, %
u = u* − β·gapunemployment rate (Okun), u* = 7, β = 0.4
π = π* + κ·gap + ρ·Δτ·100 + ω·100·(xr − xr₋₁)/xr₋₁inflation (Phillips, plus tax and import-price pass-through), π* = 2
Read on the model; since v1.2 inflation feeds the price level Prel = Prel₋₁·(1 + π/100)/(1 + π*/100) — the price level relative to the reference path (world prices grow at π*) — which enters next year's trade. No inertia: inflation itself only depends on this year's gap and pass-throughs. A consumption-tax rise is passed into prices once (ρ = 0.6); a devaluation of 10 % adds 1.5 points of inflation (ω = 0.15) the year it happens. GDP +1 % buys −0.4 point of unemployment and costs +0.3 point of inflation.

Capital flows, the exchange-rate regime and market pressure model v1.3 only

Classes on model v1.3 only (Control → Parameters; v1.1 and v1.2 classes have no capital flows and a fixed rate). The block follows Godley & Lavoie's chapter 12, reduced to what the game needs: the rest of the world holds union bills, the union's households hold foreign bills, and the regime is the administrator's choice for each year.

Bw$ = Bw$*·(1 + χw·(r − r_w) − χs·s̄)·(1 + flows/100) ; Bw = xr·Bw$the rest of the world's desired holdings of union bills (world currency): more when the union pays more than the world rate, less when the union's average spread rises or after a sudden stop (shock flows)
Bw_i = Bw · size_i·e^(−ψ·s_i) / Σ_j size_j·e^(−ψ·s_j)allocation across members: flight to quality away from members paying a spread
λF = λF0 + λf1·(r_w − r − s_i) ; xr·Bf = λF·Vhouseholds' foreign bills, a share of wealth carved out of their bills (static exchange-rate expectations)
V = V₋₁ + YD − CE + (xr − xr₋₁)·Bf₋₁wealth: the capital gain on foreign bills when the currency moves (not income)
s_i = φ·max(0, B_i,₋₁/Y_i,₋₁ − d*)risk premium of member i: 1 pt per 20 points of debt ratio above d* = 110 % of GDP; paid on the bills held by households, banks and the rest of the world — not on the central bank's (the backstop)
CA′ = Σ_i (XR_i − xr·IMR_i) + r_w,₋₁·xr·ΣBf₋₁ − Σ_i (r₋₁ + s_i,₋₁)·Bw_i,₋₁current account incl. primary income (interest received on foreign bills, paid to foreign holders)
KA = ΔBw − xr·ΔBffinancial account: net inflows
fixed: R = R₋₁ + (CA′ + KA)/xrthe reserves absorb the balance of payments (alert, crisis vote, forced devaluation as before)
float: R = R₋₁ ; xr such that CA′ + KA = 0the reserves are frozen; the exchange rate clears the balance of payments (solved inside the year with output)
Mundell–Fleming, stock-flow style. Under a floating rate, a rate rise of 0.5 pt pulls capital in and appreciates the currency by about 3.7 % on impact: import prices fall (inflation −0.5 pt at once), competitiveness and investment fall (GDP −0.9 % the next year). A sudden stop of 30 % of the rest of the world's holdings depreciates the currency by 21 %, adds 2.8 pt of inflation and improves the current account from the next year. Under a fixed rate the same flows go through the reserves: a sudden stop of 5 % costs a fifth of the stock, a voted rate cut of 0.5 pt a quarter, and a sudden stop of 30 % empties it — the trilemma in one line.

Why 3 months of reserves and cross-holdings of 15 % of GDP. The first calibration (1.5 months, i.e. 1.9 % of GDP, against gross cross-holdings of 30 % of GDP) made a voted rate cut empty the reserves within a year and a devaluation refill them to 300 % through portfolio repatriation: the direction is chapter 12's, the magnitude was unplayable. From v1.3 on the reserves are back to 3 months and the cross-holdings halved, so that portfolio moves and trade flows are of the same order.

Market pressure. A member whose debt passes 110 % of GDP pays a spread on its privately held bills; foreign holders move away from it. With the backstop on, the central bank keeps buying the residual at the common rate and the spread stays small (0.6 pt after 8 years of a +10/+10 stimulus by France). With the backstop off (administrator shock), the member's bill rate rises until households and banks absorb what the central bank no longer buys: 2 pt after 8 years, a public balance 0.6 pt worse, and the union's reserves drained by the flight.

Switching regimes. Fixed → float: the rate becomes endogenous, the reserves freeze at their last level. Float → fixed: the parity is the last floating value (a devaluation shock may be added the same year), the reserves move again.

Emissions and damages model v2.0 only

Classes on model v2.0 only (Control → Parameters). Everything above still holds; this block adds a physical side — emissions, a global stock and a damage function — two levers (carbon tax, green public investment) and a sixth objective dimension, emissions, whose weight is 0 until an election or the administrator gives it one.

E = ci·Yemissions of a member (reference carbon intensity ci* = 1, so E* = Y*)
E_w = E_w*·(1 + worldEmissions/100)rest of the world: as much as the union at the reference, exogenous
S = S₋₁ + Σ E + E_wglobal pollution stock, no natural decay; S₀ = 50 years of reference emissions (2 % a year)
D = min(0.25, 0.5·(S₋₁ − S₀)/S₀ + disaster/100)damage of the year from the stock accumulated so far (+ a disaster shock): ≈ 1 point a year under business as usual
ci = max(0.3, ci₋₁·(1 − 0.04·ctax₋₁ − 0.05·green₋₁/(1 + green₋₁/5)))carbon intensity falls persistently with last year's levers, floor 30 % of the reference; the green lever has diminishing returns (2 units −7 %, 5 units −12.5 %, 10 units −17 % a year)
u = u* + 0.4·100·D − β·gapstructural unemployment rises with the damage
π = π* + κ·gap_D + … + 0.15·Δctaxinflation on the damaged potential Y*(1 − D), plus the carbon-tax pass-through
GREEN = 0.002·green·Y* ; IM_green = (0.60 − μ)·GREENgreen investment: public spending (demand, deficit) with a 60 % import content — the excess over the economy's propensity (30 %) leaks abroad at once (to partners and the rest of the world)
Tc = 0.002·ctax·Ecarbon-tax revenue, paid by firms out of distributed income (1 point ≈ 0.2 % of GDP)
Compressed time. The reference path is no longer stationary: with nobody acting, the stock grows, the damage reaches 5 % of potential output in year 6 and 7 % in year 8 — structural unemployment +2 pt (+2.3 with Okun), inflation +1.3 pt for every member; the extra inflation erodes competitiveness, so GDP drifts down slowly (−0.9 % by year 6). The report isolates it in a fourth part of the split, « of which the climate ».

Two levers, one public good. A carbon tax of 5 points everywhere cuts the union's emissions by 70 % within five years and the year-8 damage from 7 to 5.5 %; it costs 0.6 pt of inflation and 0.7 % of GDP in the first year and brings 0.4 pt of revenue. Green investment is a relaunch with a trade-off: 60 % of it is imported equipment and its gain on the carbon intensity shrinks as you raise it. France alone at 10 units (2 pt of GDP): GDP +1.7 % in year 1 against +2.4 % for public demand +10, trade balance −1.1 against −0.7 pt, public balance −1.3 pt, emissions −15 % in year 2, −52 % by year 5. Everywhere at once, the intra-union leak comes back as partners' exports (union GDP +2.4 %) and the rest-of-the-world share still leaks. A member acting alone pays the whole cost and gets a seventh of the benefit — the rest of the world emits as much as the union, so even a united union only slows the stock.

Shocks. World emissions (%, the rest of the world's flow) and climate disaster (one-off extra damage, in points of potential output).

Duel mode classes of kind “duel” only

An arcade variant: two identical countries, A and B, each the full v2.0 economy above with its own currency and its own central bank, trading only with each other — the partner replaces the rest of the world. A class holds several pairs played in parallel; every pair is its own world, the administrator's shocks of the year hit every pair.

xr_A = e, xr_B = 1/ee = A's currency per unit of B's; both at 1 at the reference
IM_A = μ·Y_A·(e·P_B/P_A)−ε, X_A = IM_BA's imports are B's exports and vice versa (relative price = the partner's price level in A's currency)
λF = λF0 + 0.25·((r_partner + s_partner) − (r + s))households of each side hold the partner's bills; the share follows the interest-rate gap, premia included
CA′_A + KA_A = ΔR_A = −ΔR_B/efixed: the deficit side loses reserves, the surplus side gains them (3 months of imports each; alert below 25 %)
CA′_A + KA_A = 0 ⇒ efloat: the bilateral rate clears the balance of payments; reserves frozen
R ≤ 0 and CA′ < 0 ⇒ e ×1.15 (or ÷1.15)forced devaluation of an exhausted side's currency, automatic, the next year (fixed regime); the administrator may also decree a devaluation of A or B
r_X = r* + rate_X, |rate_X| ≤ 2seventh lever, per player: the policy rate, no vote; acts on investment (one-year lag), debt service and capital flows
S = S₋₁ + E_A + E_Bclosed climate: no rest of the world; otherwise as v2.0
What differs from the union. No common central bank, no rate vote, no council, no warm-up: each side has its central bank, its reserves, its risk premium and its backstop. Competitiveness is the own price level against the partner's in a common currency; inflation at home is a loss of competitiveness for you and a gain for the partner. The « of which » split has four parts: your own policy, your partner's policy, the shocks, the climate.

Orders of magnitude. Fixed rate, A alone spends +10 % in year 1: GDP A +2.7 %, GDP B +1.0 % (through A's imports); A's reserves 93, 84, 74, 64, 52, 40, 28 % of the initial stock → alert in year 8 (3 months of reserves; cross-holdings 15 % of GDP). Floating: the same stimulus depreciates A's currency by 3.9 % in year 1 and 23 % by year 5. A raising its policy rate by 1 point under a float appreciates its currency by ≈ 7 %: GDP A +1.8 % then −1.7 %, B −1.9 % then +0.3 %. A carbon tax of 5 points in A alone cuts A's emissions by 70 % in five years and the shared damage from 7 to 5.5 %: A pays, both benefit.

Shocks (every pair): confidence, import propensity, demand shock in A / in B (public demand, to break the symmetry), regime, devaluation of A / of B, central-bank backstop, climate disaster.

Leaving the union models v1.3 and v2.0, when the administrator allows it

A member may announce its exit with its decisions of a year; it leaves the union on the first day of the next year. The exit is irreversible. Several members may leave in the same game; each gets its own currency.

at the exitevery stock of the country is converted 1 for 1 into its new currency — cash, deposits, loans and all its public debt, including the part held by the rest of the world; its households' foreign bills stay in world currency
R_X = R·Y*_X / ΣY*its new central bank receives a share of the union's reserves by size, takes over its cash H and its banks' advances A
BcbU = Bcbthe union's central bank keeps the country's bills it held (now in the new currency)
T2 = A + xr·R_X − HTARGET balance, in euros, that closes both balance sheets (> 0: the country's central bank owes the union). At the reference T2 = −Bcb: the union owes the country exactly what it keeps of its bills
Eprice of one euro in the new currency (1 at the exit; up = depreciation); against the rest of the world the rate is E·xr: the country chooses only E
float: CA′(E) + KA(E) = 0E clears its balance of payments each year, its reserves are frozen
peg: R_X = R_X,−1 + (CA′ + KA)/(E·xr)its reserves absorb the balance; the parity moves only when the country decides (±20 %) or when its reserves are exhausted with a deficit (forced devaluation of 15 %)
r_X = r* + rateits own policy rate (±2 pt), on investment next year, on its debt and on the capital flows; it no longer votes at the union's council
π_X = … + ω·Δ(E·xr) + ω·((1 − sR)/sR)·ΔEall its imports now go through the exchange rate: a depreciation of 10 % against the euro adds about 3 pt of inflation that year
tradepriced in the producer's currency, as with the rest of the world; relative prices in a common currency, with a one-year lag
What it costs, what it buys (France, model v1.3, years 2–5 after an announcement in year 1). Its own rate: a cut of 1 pt depreciates the new currency by 11 %, adds 2.6 pt of inflation and costs 2.3 % of GDP the first year (the import bill rises before volumes react), then GDP is 1.4 % above the reference. A permanent stimulus of +5: in a float the currency slides (+2 % → +11 % over four years) and inflation stays 0.8–1.0 pt higher; in a peg the reserves drain (90, 78, 64, 47, 29 % of the share received). A devaluation of 10 % in a peg: inflation +2.4 pt, the reserves refill by revaluation.

What the others can do. The administrator may hit the exiting country only: household confidence (−3 % → GDP −1.3 %), a sale of its bills by the rest of the world (−20 % for a year → currency −20 %, inflation +4.7 pt), a deposit run into foreign bills — the year of the announcement it is paid out of the union's common reserves (1 pt of wealth costs the union 12 % of its stock). The remaining members, in a council on retaliation held before the decisions of the exit year (and again whenever the administrator convenes it), vote the median level of a tariff on its exports (10 %: GDP −1.9 %, the union's reserves rise) and of a yearly sale of its bills held by their central bank (half of them: currency −12 %, bill rate +0.2 pt; buying them back spares the rate, not the currency), and, if its central bank owes the union, the immediate settlement of its TARGET balance.

Who pays for the past. The union's net claim on the leaver — its bills kept plus the TARGET balance — equals the leaver's cumulative balance-of-payments deficit beyond its share of the union's: zero at the reference, 4.9 % of its GDP after a deposit run of 3 pt of wealth while still a member.

Tariffs when the administrator switches them on

A customs union has one external tariff: the members vote it each year, and a country that has left the union may tax the union's goods. All tariffs are 0 unless switched on.

IMR_i·(1 + cet)−εa member's imports from the rest of the world under the common external tariff (this year's rate)
cet·xr·IMR_irevenue of the importing government, paid by its households: C = (1 − τc)·CE − revenue
cet = median of the voteseach member votes 0, 5, 10 or 20 % with its decisions; no vote, no change
XR_i·(1 + wt)−ηthe rest of the world's tariff on the members' exports (administrator shock)
(1 + tariffU)a country out of the union taxes what it buys from the members (0–20 %), like the union's retaliation tariff
A tax on your own consumers. A common external tariff of 10 % brings every member about 1.4 % of GDP of revenue and improves the trade balance (+1.1 pt) and the reserves, but GDP falls by 0.6 %: households pay the tariff, while imports from the rest of the world fall by only 7 %. If the rest of the world answers with 10 % on the union's exports, the trade gain is gone and GDP falls by 2 %. A country out of the union that taxes the union's goods at 10 % gains revenue (1.6 pt of GDP) and some GDP (+0.8 %), but its currency appreciates and gives part of it back; the union loses about 0.6 % of GDP.

How many members?

The union of a class is made of the countries its groups govern — from two to seven, chosen before the game starts. Smaller unions keep the same equations and reference ratios: trade shares, reserves and the rest of the world's holdings scale with the members' sizes. Each member then weighs more in its partners' trade and in the common reserves: a French stimulus of 10 % lifts its partners' GDP by 0.14 % in the union of seven, 0.35 % in a union with Italy and Spain only.

The rules around the model: votes and the reserves crisis

Rate voteWith its decisions of the year, each country votes cut, hold or hike; one country, one vote. A majority of the votes cast (hold counts as a vote; a group that does not submit does not vote) moves the rate by 0.5 point, permanently (the changes add up over the years), on top of the administrator's rate shock; bounded between 0 and 10 %. Secret while the round is open.
AlertChecked at the end of each year: reserves below 25 % of the initial stock.
Crisis voteWhen the union ends a year under alert, the next year opens with a vote, before any decision: austerity (every member must keep public demand and benefits at least 5 % below the reference that year) or devaluation (10 %). Devaluation needs a strict majority of the playing countries; a group that does not vote does not count. The result is announced, then decisions open.
Forced devaluationReserves at or below zero and a current-account deficit at the end of the previous year: a devaluation of 15 % is applied, without a vote. Once the current account is back in surplus, the union is repaying its foreign borrowing and the ordinary crisis vote applies again.
Currency crisis (v1.3, floating)No reserves crisis while floating. If the currency fell by more than 10 % over the previous year, the next year opens with a vote, before any decision: defend (the central bank raises its rate by 1 pt for the year, on top of the rate vote and the shocks) or let it float. A country that does not vote counts as defend; letting the currency float needs more than half of the playing countries.
Regime and shocks (v1.3)The exchange-rate regime, the capital-flow shock (sudden stop), the world interest rate and the central-bank backstop are set by the administrator year by year in the shocks editor, like the other shocks; a persistent sudden stop must be repeated each year.
Leaving the union (§12)Allowed by the administrator (models v1.3 and v2.0). A country announces its exit with its decisions of year N; it is out from year N+1, has its own policy rate and chooses each year a floating or pegged currency. It no longer votes at the council. In year N+1, before any decision, the remaining members hold a council on retaliation: tariff on its exports (0, 5, 10 or 20 %), yearly sale of its bills held by the union's central bank (0, 25, 50 or 100 %) and, if it owes the union, settlement of its TARGET balance. Outcome: the median of the members' votes (absent = 0); measures in force until a later council changes them.
ObjectiveR = 0.2·ΔGDP − 0.2·Δu − 0.2·Δπ + 0.2·ΔPB + 0.2·ΔTB: the score of the year (deviations from the reference: GDP in %, the rest in points). The ranking uses the cumulative R, the sum of the yearly scores. Elections: the administrator may change these weights for one country or for the whole union from a given year on (a new government, new voters’ priorities); the weights in force are shown on your page and stored with each year’s result. Model v2.0 adds a sixth dimension, emissions (your emissions in % of the reference, counted negatively): its weight is 0 by default — the damage already shows through unemployment and inflation, as a common pool — and it becomes part of R only when an election makes it a priority (preset « Green: emissions first »: emissions 0.40, the five others 0.12, for one country or union-wide) or when the administrator sets its weight in the parameters. Until then, cutting emissions is a public good: it costs the country that does it and benefits everyone.
